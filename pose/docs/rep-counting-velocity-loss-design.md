## Problem Statement

Turn the already-working pose-tracking + state-classification pipeline (`pose_coach/`, merged and demo-verified on real hardware) into the actual training-coach behavior: count sit-to-stand reps, detect within-set fatigue via velocity loss, and recommend load progression between sessions — all per-participant, without demographic profiling or calibration, per the two prior artifacts this design builds directly on:

- **Step-1 design doc** (`lucasting-main-design-20260729-004848.md`): pose tracking + SEATED/STANDING/TRANSITIONING/UNKNOWN state classification, 3 review rounds, implemented and merged into `pose_coach/` on this branch.
- **Velocity-loss research doc** (`pose/docs/velocity-loss-decision-research.md`, this repo): resolved the individualization problem (self-referential decisions beat demographic profiling), decided the 20% fatigue-stop threshold, and specified the progression mechanism (per-participant rolling baseline). 3 review rounds, all citations independently verified.

This is the layer that makes the device actually coach, not just watch.

## What Makes This Cool

The pose tracker already proved it can watch someone's body in real time with zero setup. This slice is where it starts *thinking* — telling a participant "you're fatiguing, stop here" and telling a facilitator "this person's ready for the next band" — using nothing but the shape of their own movement getting slower, compared only against their own history. No two participants get the same numbers plugged into the same formula; every decision is relative to that specific person, which is the whole reason this can work across wildly different bodies without ever asking "how old are you" or "how frail are you."

## Constraints

- **No identity fields, ever** (velocare `CLAUDE.md` hard invariant #2) — all persistence keys on the existing pseudonymous `participant_id` convention (e.g. `P-0042`), never a name or any PII.
- **No clinical output** (velocare `CLAUDE.md` hard invariant #3) — outputs are training instructions (`step up` / `hold` / `step down`, matching velocare's own already-decided UI language for the open load-progression-mechanism decision), never a risk score or clinical label.
- **Load progression mechanism is an open decision** (velocare `CLAUDE.md`, "Open decisions"): resistance band vs. chair height isn't settled. This design treats "band" as an abstract ordered ladder, per velocare's own instruction: **never hardcode band colors or a specific mechanism.**
- **Velocity-intent validity is a named open risk in velocare's own `CLAUDE.md`**: *"Where a session's rep-to-rep velocity is erratic rather than monotonically declining, prefer declining to make a recommendation over making a weak one."* This is the same principle the velocity-loss research doc arrived at independently (the first-3-rep consistency check, Section 5) — cross-validated by two independent design passes, and treated as a hard requirement here, not a nice-to-have.
- No calibration, no video/image persistence, no clinical/diagnostic language — same constraints as step 1, still in force.
- Deadline: PHIT preliminary proposal deadline is imminent — this slice should be demoable, not production-hardened.

## Premises

1. **Rep counting = counting SEATED→STANDING→SEATED transitions from the existing state machine.** No new tracking logic. A rep starts on leaving SEATED, its concentric phase ends on reaching STANDING (where velocity is measured), and it's only counted once the participant returns to SEATED (full cycle complete). — **Agreed by user.**
   - **Discard rules (revised twice after review — see below for why the first revision was still wrong).** A rep is discarded (not counted, not logged) if: (a) **any** `UNKNOWN` frame occurs during its concentric phase, full stop — no single-frame exemption; or (b) the concentric phase duration is implausibly short (placeholder: <0.3s — not a physically plausible sit-to-stand for any population, tune against real footage like every other threshold in this project).
   - **Why there's no single-frame `UNKNOWN` exemption, even though one seems more forgiving:** an earlier revision tried tolerating single-frame blips and discarding only on a *sustained* gap, keying the discard decision off whether `side_used` changed. Checking the actual pipeline code (`pipeline.py`'s `_unknown_output`) showed this doesn't hold: **every** `UNKNOWN` frame — including a same-side, single-frame occlusion blip — unconditionally resets `_last_side` to `None`, which forces a full smoothing-buffer flush (`SideSmoothingState.reset()`, clearing both the `knee_angle` and `hip_height_norm` buffers together) on the next valid frame, even when the physical side never changed. Worse, `side_used` on the `UNKNOWN` frame itself is sometimes reported as the real side label (degenerate-geometry path) and sometimes as `None` (no-person path) — inconsistently, making "did `side_used` change" an unreliable proxy for "did a flush happen" either way. Since this project's whole design philosophy (median smoothing, hysteresis) exists specifically to avoid fabricated discontinuities, and a "tolerated" blip turns out to reintroduce exactly that discontinuity in a realistic live-demo scenario (momentary occlusion mid-rep), the simpler and actually-correct rule is: any `UNKNOWN` frame during the concentric phase discards the rep. This trades a higher discard rate (possible undercounting on flaky tracking) for actual correctness on the reps that do get counted — the right tradeoff for a device whose numbers feed a fatigue/safety decision. If undercounting proves to be a real problem against real footage, the more surgical fix is exposing a "smoothing buffer was just flushed" signal from the pipeline itself — a step-1 change, out of this slice's scope, noted here as a future option rather than attempted now.
   - `range_of_motion`/`min_angle`/`max_angle` are scoped to the **concentric phase only** (SEATED-exit to STANDING-entry, same window as velocity) — not the full SEATED→STANDING→SEATED rep cycle. This is a deliberate scope choice, not just a labeling detail: it's what lets the same "any `UNKNOWN` during the concentric phase" discard rule protect ROM the identical way it protects velocity, via the identical buffer-flush mechanism. Scoping ROM to the full rep cycle instead would need its own separate discard rule covering the return phase too — deferred, not needed for this slice's decisions (fatigue-stop and progression only consume concentric-phase data).
   - **Failed-stand attempts** (SEATED→TRANSITIONING→SEATED, never reaching STANDING) are not counted as a rep, but are logged as a distinct `failed_stand_count` on the `SetSummary` — plausibly a more important safety signal in elder care than velocity loss on completed reps, so it's tracked rather than silently dropped.
2. **Rep velocity is derived from the already-computed `hip_height_norm` signal** during the concentric (SEATED-exit → STANDING-entry) phase — no new landmark math, reuses step 1's calibration-free, `pose_world_landmarks`-based measurement. — **Agreed by user.**
   - **Revised after review — do not naively differentiate the smoothed per-frame stream.** `hip_height_norm` on `FrameOutput` is already median-filtered (5-frame window, per step 1) for outlier robustness; median filters produce a stepwise/quantized signal, and framewise differencing of that signal produces derivative artifacts at each step boundary — a real numerical problem, not a style choice. **Fix:** `mean_velocity` for a rep is computed as *whole-phase displacement over time* — `(hip_height_norm at STANDING-entry − hip_height_norm at SEATED-exit) / concentric_time` — not a per-frame derivative. This sidesteps the artifact entirely, and is exactly the quantity `velocity_loss_pct` needs (a ratio of two mean_velocity values computed identically, so any residual smoothing bias mostly cancels in the ratio). **Endpoint-sampling convention:** both "SEATED-exit" and "STANDING-entry" are sampled at the *first frame classified into the new state* — a convention this design defines for itself, not an existing pattern in `state_machine.py` (which just does plain per-frame classification with no built-in transition-frame concept) — applied consistently to both endpoints so any one-frame offset affects numerator and `concentric_time` the same way. **Ordering:** the implausible-short-duration discard check (Premise 1) runs *before* the velocity division, not after — `state_machine.py` has no minimum-frame-count hysteresis on state transitions, so a single frame could in principle satisfy STANDING immediately after leaving SEATED, making `concentric_time` zero; the duration guard must be checked first to avoid a division by zero, not just to filter noise. `peak_velocity` is computed as the maximum single-frame delta of the (already-smoothed) `hip_height_norm` series within the concentric phase — an explicitly known-limitation estimate: it can be *damped* during steady smoothing, or *spiked* if it happens to land right after a tracking-gap recovery (not a one-directional bias). This is acceptable because **`peak_velocity` doesn't drive any decision in this design** (only `mean_velocity`/`velocity_loss_pct` gate fatigue-stop and progression) — it's captured for schema completeness (matching the brief's own field list) but isn't load-bearing, which lowers the stakes of the approximation considerably.
3. **Fatigue-stop threshold: 20% velocity loss** (first-3 vs. last-3 rep mean velocity, per the research doc's Section 1 — bodyweight-modality and camera-noise-floor justified, not directly inherited from the 10%-threshold loaded-machine study). — **Agreed by user.**
4. **Progression: per-participant rolling baseline**, not a fixed target zone — median of trailing 3 sessions at a band, 10% promotion margin, must persist across 2 consecutive sessions, cold-start = hold (no population default substituted), regression = never-auto-reduce interim default. Per the research doc's Section 2. — **Agreed by user.**
5. **Scope boundary — build standalone, don't guess at `SessionDataSource`.** velocare's `CLAUDE.md` says the pose pipeline will eventually implement a `SessionDataSource` interface owned by the frontend work, which this session hasn't seen. This slice designs its own data model and decision functions on their own terms, and flags interface conformance as an explicit follow-up coordination item with whoever owns that interface — not something to guess at now. — **Agreed by user.**
6. **What already exists / reused, not rebuilt:** the entire `pose_coach` pure-logic pipeline (state classification, hysteresis, smoothing) from step 1 — this slice only adds a consumer of its per-frame output stream, no changes to that pipeline itself. The brief's own Level 2 per-rep/per-set data schema (section 4.4: `rep_index`, `concentric_time`, `peak_velocity`, `mean_velocity`, `range_of_motion`, `form_flags`, `first3_mean_velocity`, `last3_mean_velocity`, `velocity_loss_pct`) is reused as this slice's field names rather than inventing new ones, extended with `min_angle`/`max_angle` (also named in the brief's schema, just not in the shorter list originally quoted here) and the new `failed_stand_count` field from Premise 1.
7. **What happens if we skip this step:** nothing changes for the participant — the device would still just be a passive tracker with a label on screen. This is the layer that turns tracking into coaching, which is the actual product.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Minimal extension of the existing pipeline
Thin `RepCounter` + `VelocityLossCalculator` bolted directly onto `PoseTrackingPipeline` outputs, in-memory only, extends `scripts/run_demo.py` directly.
- Pros: fastest to build, directly demoable, reuses the already-tested pipeline as-is.
- Cons: progression's rolling baseline needs *some* persistence to be real, not simulated — so "minimal" doesn't actually avoid the persistence question, it just does it ad hoc. Likely needs restructuring the moment multi-session/multi-participant use is added, which is immediately (progression is inherently cross-session).

### Approach B: Modular decision-engine package (recommended)
New `pose_coach/autoregulation/` package: `rep_counter.py`, `velocity.py`, `fatigue_stop.py`, `progression.py`, and a pluggable `session_store.py` (starts as JSON-file-per-participant, swappable later without a rewrite).
- Pros: mirrors step 1's proven pure/impure split (testability paid off there — caught 2 real bugs via unit/sequence tests); persistence abstraction avoids a rewrite when real storage or `SessionDataSource` conformance lands; scales cleanly to multi-participant/multi-band without restructuring.
- Cons: more files/structure than the bare minimum; slightly more upfront design than a quick script.

### Approach C: Event-sourced rep log
Every rep is an immutable logged event; fatigue-stop/progression are pure reducers folding over the event log.
- Pros: every decision traceable back to raw logged reps (valuable for a safety-relevant device — "why did it stop the set" has a real answer); the 20%-vs-10%/10%-margin threshold tuning question becomes a replay against logged history, not a re-run.
- Cons: more sophistication than a 2-day hackathon slice needs; unfamiliar pattern risks losing time to the pattern itself, not the actual problem.

## Recommended Approach

**Approach B**, chosen by the user. Matches the deadline, mirrors the architecture that already proved itself in step 1, and avoids Approach A's false economy (persistence is needed either way) without reaching for Approach C's sophistication before it's earned.

## Data Model

Reusing the brief's own Level 2 schema field names (section 4.4) directly:

**Per rep** (`RepRecord`): `rep_index`, `concentric_time` (s), `peak_velocity`, `mean_velocity` (both per the revised Premise 2 computation), `range_of_motion` (°, from `knee_angle_smoothed` range — same signal already driving state classification, not a second raw-based path), `min_angle`, `max_angle`, `form_flags`, `timestamp`. Discard rules per the revised Premise 1.

**`form_flags` — deferred, not designed here.** Step 1 computes `trunk_lean` and hip angle but (by its own design, Approach A) never gates on them — no threshold exists anywhere in this project yet for what counts as a flaggable trunk lean or asymmetry. Rather than inventing an ungrounded number, `form_flags` is captured as an empty/reserved field in this slice and moved to Open Questions — implementing it needs its own short design pass (probably reusing the same hysteresis-banded-threshold pattern already established twice in this project), not a placeholder guessed here.

**Set boundaries — minimal standalone definition, since `SessionDataSource` is out of scope (Premise 5).** A set is bounded by explicit `start_set()`/`end_set()` calls on the autoregulation module — for the standalone demo, `run_demo.py` itself calls `start_set()` on launch and `end_set()` on quit (or after a fixed rep count, whichever the demo script chooses). In production this would come from a facilitator action in the frontend; that wiring is part of the already-flagged `SessionDataSource` follow-up, not solved here.

**Per set** (`SetSummary`): `exercise_id`, `load_label` (abstract band identifier, per the open-decision constraint — never a hardcoded color), `rep_count`, `failed_stand_count`, `first3_mean_velocity`, `last3_mean_velocity` (trailing-3 window, can overlap with first3 for short sets — matches the brief's own definition), `velocity_loss_pct`, `fatigue_stop_triggered` (tri-state: `True`/`False`/`None` — see consistency-guard note below), `mean_rom`, `flag_count`, **`baseline_consistency_ok`** (bool — the coefficient-of-variation guard on first-3 reps).

**Consistency guard applies to both decisions, not just progression (corrected after review).** The guard's actual source — the research doc's Section 5, titled "Edge case: unreliable baseline signal (**within a set**)" — and velocare `CLAUDE.md`'s "prefer declining to make a recommendation over making a weak one" are both fundamentally about the within-set fatigue-stop decision; progression was the secondary extension, not the primary case. An earlier revision of this doc wired the guard only to `recommended_load_next`, leaving `fatigue_stop_triggered` free to fire off a `velocity_loss_pct` computed from a baseline the design's own cited sources say shouldn't be trusted — a real gap in exactly the safety-relevant path. **Fix:** when `baseline_consistency_ok` is `False`, `fatigue_stop_triggered` is set to `None` (not `False`) — an explicit "cannot assess via velocity" state, distinct from "assessed as not fatigued," surfaced to the facilitator UI as a flag (reusing the existing `flag_count`/`form_flags` mechanism) rather than silently defaulting to either a stop or a non-stop the system doesn't actually have grounds for. `recommended_load_next` is still forced to `hold` in this case too.

**Minimum-rep-count fallback:** below 3 completed reps (`rep_count` 0, 1, or 2 — including the all-failed-stand case, `rep_count=0` with `failed_stand_count>0`, which is itself a safety-relevant signal worth surfacing even with no velocity data at all), `first3_mean_velocity`/`last3_mean_velocity`/`velocity_loss_pct`/`mean_rom` are `None` and `recommended_load_next` is forced to `hold` — there isn't enough data to compute anything else, and this is exactly the kind of "prefer no recommendation over a weak one" situation velocare's `CLAUDE.md` already names.

**Per participant, per band** (persisted via `session_store`): rolling history of `SetSummary.first3_mean_velocity` per session, keyed by pseudonymous `participant_id` — no other field ever contains identity data. **Not currently keyed by `exercise_id`** — this slice targets sit-to-stand only (matching step 1 and the brief's initial scope, which hasn't yet reached multiple exercises), so the key is `(participant_id, band)`; `exercise_id` is carried on `SetSummary` for future-proofing but isn't part of the persistence key yet. Revisit when a second exercise is added. **Trailing-median semantics:** the median of the trailing 3 sessions is always recomputed fresh from whatever is currently stored (not frozen at the time of a prior promotion decision) — simpler than versioning historical snapshots, and matches how every other rolling computation in this project already works.

**Output**: `recommended_load_next` ∈ `{step_up, hold, step_down}` (velocare's own already-decided UI language) — `step_down` is not yet reachable (regression trigger is undefined, interim default is never-auto-reduce, per the research doc), so this slice only ever emits `step_up` or `hold` until that's formalized. This enum is the natural seam for a future `SessionDataSource` adapter — not built now, flagged in Open Questions.

## Success Criteria

- Given a synthetic sequence of `FrameOutput` state values (reusing step 1's test-fixture style — no camera/model needed), the rep counter correctly counts complete SEATED→STANDING→SEATED cycles and discards incomplete ones (tracking gap mid-rep).
- Given synthetic rep sequences, `velocity_loss_pct` computes correctly and `fatigue_stop_triggered` fires at the right rep for known first-3/last-3 velocity inputs, including the trailing-window overlap case for short sets.
- Given a synthetic multi-session history at a band, the progression logic correctly holds at cold-start (<3 sessions), correctly requires 2-consecutive-session persistence before recommending `step_up`, and correctly forces `hold` when the consistency guard fails on a session's first-3 reps regardless of the raw velocity numbers.
- Given a synthetic set with fewer than 3 completed reps (including an all-failed-stand set), all velocity/ROM fields are `None`, `recommended_load_next` is `hold`, and `failed_stand_count` is still correctly reported.
- Given a synthetic set with a high-variance (inconsistent) first-3 reps, `baseline_consistency_ok` is `False`, `fatigue_stop_triggered` is `None` (not `False`) and flagged, and `recommended_load_next` is `hold` — the consistency guard must be shown to gate *both* decisions, not just progression.
- Given a synthetic rep sequence containing a single-frame `UNKNOWN` blip mid-concentric-phase, that rep is discarded (not counted, not logged) — regression test for the discard-rule revision above.
- No participant identity data appears anywhere in the data model or persistence layer — every stored record is keyed only by pseudonymous `participant_id`.
- `recommended_load_next` never emits anything other than `step_up`/`hold` (never `step_down`) until the regression trigger is formalized — enforced by a test, not just by omission.
- Real-webcam validation (extending the step-1 demo script) shows a live rep counter incrementing correctly during an actual sit-to-stand set, with a visible velocity-loss readout.
